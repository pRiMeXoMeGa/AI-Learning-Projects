# 04 — Niche Analysis: Which Niche Is Most Attractive?

## Scoring method

Each niche is scored 1–5 on eight criteria (5 is best). The weights reflect
**your** constraints: a 2–5 person technical team, a $1k–10k budget, B2C, a
global English-first market and a VC-scale goal.

| Criterion | Weight | Why this weight |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|
| Growth evidence (2026 revenue traction of leaders) | 20% | You asked for real, attractive growth above everything else |
| Willingness to pay | 15% | Consumers must pay quickly; no runway for a long free period |
| Retention potential | 15% | AI apps churn ~36% faster (RevenueCat). Retention decides whether you can raise |
| Budget fit (inference cost per user) | 15% | A $1k–10k runway |
| Foundation-lab risk (5 = labs unlikely to absorb it) | 10% | The wrapper risk |
| Competition (5 = open space) | 10% | Ease of breaking through |
| Regulatory risk (5 = low risk) | 5% | Legal cost and reputational exposure |
| VC-scale potential | 10% | Plausible path to $100M+ ARR |

### Notes on the scores

- **Creator video** has the strongest growth (Higgsfield ~$700M ARR) but scores low on budget fit (each clip costs $2–12 to generate) and on competition (Higgsfield, Arcads, Creatify, HeyGen, Captions, OpusClip). It becomes attractive in a **vertical** where the leaders aren't focused. That's idea 2.
- **Vibe coding** shows huge growth but is the most crowded niche with the highest lab risk (the labs ship coding agents themselves) and doubtful consumer retention. Avoid.
- **Companions and therapy** can retain users but carry real regulatory and safety risk (SB 243, the Illinois ban). A poor fit for a small team.
- **Photo editing** has been commoditized by the labs (Nano Banana). Avoid.
- **Kids' learning** scores well but COPPA and child voice data raise compliance cost. It's a strong alternate if you have edtech or child-safety expertise.
- **Social connection** has the best moat (network effects that labs can't copy) and strong VC interest (Overtone's $18M seed), but the cold-start problem and operations load are heavy for $1k–10k. An alternate.

## Conclusion: the most attractive niche

### Voice-first AI coaching for skills with an economic payoff

**Why it wins for you:**

1. **Proven willingness to pay.** Speak reached ~$100M ARR and a $1B+ valuation in voice tutoring. ElevenLabs (~$600M ARR) and Wispr ($2B valuation) show voice is the breakout modality of 2026.
2. **Cheap to run.** Real-time voice now costs ~$0.016–0.05 per minute. A typical user (~150 min/month) costs ~$2.50–7.50 to serve against a ~$20/month price.
3. **Retention is built in.** Skill progress is measurable (scores, streaks, before-and-after recordings). This directly targets the AI-app churn problem.
4. **Defensible against the labs.** ChatGPT voice can chat, but it doesn't provide structured scenario libraries, rubric scoring, progress analytics or career-specific content. The data you gather (where real users struggle) compounds over time.
5. **A big market.** ~1.5B English speakers with non-native speakers outnumbering native ~3:1; a ~$23B business-English training market; a ~$16B digital English market. Investors already value the category (Speak).
6. **Matches a 2–5 person team.** Real-time audio, evaluation pipelines and a mobile app are hard enough to keep casual competitors out, but achievable for your team.
7. **Low regulatory risk** for adult skill coaching.

**Main risks:** Speak moving into professional English; ChatGPT and Gemini
voice improving; the need for sharp positioning (a specific outcome, not
"practise English").
